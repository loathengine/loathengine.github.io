Key points

  • Almost every commercial bottleneck rifle propellant sold worldwide originates in just eight industrial OEM chemical plants across North America, Europe, and Australia.
  • Commercial canister brands act primarily as specification managers, ballistic laboratories, packagers, and retail distributors—often sourcing from three or four different OEM plants under one corporate umbrella.
  • Because nitrocellulose nitration plants depend on defense ammunition contracts for capital scale, military demand surges directly tighten commercial canister availability.
  • Two decades of consolidation—especially Hodgdon’s acquisitions of IMR (2003), the Winchester powder license (2005), and Western Powders (2020)—concentrated North American retail distribution even while global chemical manufacturing stayed specialized.

OEM Chemical Plants Versus Commercial Packagers

A widespread misconception in handloading is that every brand name printed on a one-pound jug operates its own smokeless powder factory. In reality, the global propellant industry is sharply divided into two tiers: industrial OEM (Original Equipment Manufacturer) chemical plants and commercial canister packagers (Figure 9-1).

Manufacturing smokeless propellant from raw cellulose requires heavy chemical infrastructure: concentrated nitric and sulfuric acid plants, acid-recovery towers, remote-controlled nitration houses, high-pressure hydraulic extrusion presses or heated spherical-slurry reactors, solvent-recovery loops, and explosive-rated drying and blending magazines.1 Environmental permitting, hazard-zone land buffers, and capital costs make building a new greenfield nitrocellulose plant virtually prohibitive for a purely civilian sporting brand. As a result, nearly all active smokeless rifle propellant plants were founded as state arsenals or defense-industrial energetics complexes between 1898 and 1970.

Commercial packagers sit downstream of those chemical plants. A packager defines target burn rates and ballistic acceptance windows, contracts bulk production runs from one or more OEMs, tests incoming master lots in pressure barrels, bottles the powder into retail canisters, publishes load manuals, and manages dealer distribution. Some OEMs—such as Nammo Vihtavuori and Nitrochemie (via Reload Swiss)—sell directly to consumers under their own factory brands, while major American packagers like Hodgdon and Alliant draw across multiple international plants to assemble complete catalogs.

That two-tier structure explains commercial supply cycles. Military small-arms and artillery contracts account for the primary tonnage at most OEM facilities. When defense procurement surges, extrusion presses and nitration lines prioritize government ammunition contracts, leaving commercial packagers queuing for bulk canister allocations.

The Eight Primary Modern OEM Plants

1. General Dynamics Ordnance and Tactical Systems – Canada (Valleyfield, Quebec)

Originally established during World War II as a major Allied explosives complex and operated for decades under Canadian Industries Limited (CIL) and later Expro Chemical Products, the Valleyfield, Quebec facility is operated today as General Dynamics Ordnance and Tactical Systems – Canada (GD-OTS Canada).2 (Older handloading literature occasionally misidentifies the plant as “Explosives Ltd,” a mangling of the former Expro name.)

Valleyfield is North America’s primary solvent-extruded single-base nitrocellulose plant. After DuPont exited direct IMR manufacturing in the late 1980s, traditional IMR extruded rifle production (IMR 3031, 4064, 4198, 4227, 4350, 4831, 4895, 7828, and 7828SSC) consolidated at Valleyfield.2 The plant also manufactured the copper-fouling-erasing IMR Enduron series (4166, 4451, 4955, 7977, 8133) prior to its August 2022 discontinuation and produced contracted single-base extruded formulations for Accurate (Accurate 2015, 2495, 4064, 4350, LT-30, and LT-32).3

2. General Dynamics St. Marks Powder (St. Marks, Florida, USA)

Constructed by Olin Corporation in 1970 to succeed its original East Alton, Illinois ball-powder works—and acquired by General Dynamics in 1999—St. Marks Powder is the world’s largest producer of spherical BALL POWDER® propellants.4 Using Fred Olsen’s water-slurry lacquer emulsion process, St. Marks forms nitrocellulose-nitroglycerin globules in heated water reactors, distills off solvent, applies deterrents, and mechanically rolls selected grades into flattened spheroids to control web thickness and burn progressivity.5

Alongside its core role supplying U.S. military 5.56mm (WC 844) and 7.62mm (WC 846) propellants, St. Marks manufactures a vast share of the North American spherical rifle market: Winchester (W748, W760, Supreme 780, StaBALL Match, StaBALL 6.5, StaBALL HD), Hodgdon spherical rifle lines (H335, BL-C(2), H380, CFE 223, CFE BLK, Leverevolution, SUPERFORMANCE, US 869), Alliant Power Pro rifle propellants (1200-R, 2000-MR, 4000-MR, Varmint), and select Accurate spherical grades.6

3. Thales Australia / Australian Munitions (Mulwala, New South Wales)

Built in 1943 as the Mulwala Explosives Factory to supply Commonwealth forces in the Pacific theater, the Mulwala plant transitioned through Australian Defence Industries (ADI) before Thales acquired full ownership in 2006 and commissioned a heavily modernized propellant facility in the 2010s.7

Mulwala is renowned for clean, tightly controlled single-base extruded rifle propellants incorporating temperature-insensitive surface coatings.8 Sold domestically across Australia and New Zealand under the ADI label (AR2206H, AR2207, AR2208, AR2209, AR2213SC, AR2217, AR2219, AR2225, BM2, BM8208), those same formulations form the backbone of Hodgdon’s Extreme extruded lineup (H4895, H4198, Varget, H4350, H4831/H4831SC, H1000, H322, Retumbo, Benchmark) as well as IMR 8208 XBR.9 Because traditional ADI deterrent chemistry relied on compounds restricted under European Union REACH Annex XIV from 2018 onward, Mulwala powders disappeared from EU retail shelves while remaining dominant in North America and Australasia.10

4. Nammo Vihtavuori Oy (Vihtavuori / Laukaa, Finland)

Founded by the Finnish government in 1922 as the State Powder Factory (Valtion Ruutitehdas), the Vihtavuori plant began smokeless powder production in 1926 to supply Finland’s military rifle ammunition.11 After periods under Kemira and Eurenco ownership, the facility became part of Nordic defense group Nammo in 2014.11

Vihtavuori is one of the few vertically integrated commercial propellant makers, producing its own nitrocellulose from Scandinavian wood pulp and cotton linters alongside finished extruded rifle powders.12 Its catalog spans the clean-burning N100 single-base series (N120 through N170), the nitroglycerin-infused N500 high-energy series (N530 through N570, including modern temperature-stabilized releases N555, N565, and N568), and .50 BMG-class 20N29 and 24N41.13 Early transition to REACH-compliant deterrents made Vihtavuori a primary beneficiary of Europe’s 2018 import restrictions on non-REACH extruded powders.10

5. Eurenco Bofors AB (Karlskoga, Sweden)

Tracing its lineage directly to AB Bofors Nobelkrut, established by Alfred Nobel’s estate in Karlskoga in 1898, Eurenco Bofors AB operates today within the European Eurenco energetics group.14 Bofors specializes in double-base and surface-deterred extruded small-arms and artillery propellants.

In the commercial bottleneck rifle market, Bofors manufactures the entire Norma rifle catalog (Norma 200, 201, 202, 203-B, 204, 217, URP, and MRP) as well as the Swedish-sourced core of the Alliant Reloder line (Reloder 7, 10x, 15, TS 15.5, 16, 19, 22, 23, 25, and AR-Comp).15 Crucially, Eurenco Bofors is the developer and trademark owner of TZ® (Temperature Zone) technology—a DNT-free, DBP-free, decoppering-capable chemical stabilization architecture used in AR-Comp, Reloder 23, Reloder 16, and Reloder TS 15.5.16

6. Nitrochemie Wimmis AG and Nitrochemie Aschau GmbH (Switzerland / Germany)

Formed in 1998 as a joint venture between Germany’s Rheinmetall (55%) and Switzerland’s RUAG (45%), Nitrochemie unites historic Swiss propellant works at Wimmis (established 1917) with German energetics facilities at Aschau.17

Nitrochemie pioneered EI® (Extruded Impregnated) propellant technology, which infuses nitroglycerin into extruded nitrocellulose grains with controlled radial concentration gradients so the charge delivers high energy density alongside progressive burning behavior.18 In 2013, Nitrochemie launched its own factory-direct consumer brand, Reload Swiss (RS36–RS80 for bottleneck rifles), while continuing to manufacture EI®-based contract propellants for Alliant Powder (Reloder 17, Reloder 26, Reloder 33, and Reloder 50).19

7. PB Clermont / Eurenco (Engis, Belgium)

Established in 1952 in Engis, Belgium (historically linked to Poudreries Réunies de Belgique) and now integrated within the Eurenco group, PB Clermont is Europe’s premier specialist in spherical (ball) smokeless propellants.20 Like St. Marks in the United States, Clermont produces water-emulsion double-base spherical grains and uses mechanical rolling mills to flatten spheres into precise web thicknesses for rifle burn-rate control.20

PB Clermont supplies the spherical rifle lines marketed in North America as Ramshot (X-Terminator, TAC, Big Game, Hunter, Magnum, LRT, and Grand) and Accurate spherical powders (Accurate 1680, 2200, 2230, 2460, 2520, 2700, and MagPro), as well as the spherical Sp series sold in Europe under Nobel Sport’s Vectan brand.21

8. Explosia a.s. (Pardubice-Semtín, Czech Republic)

Founded in 1920 at Pardubice-Semtín as a state-backed Czechoslovakian explosives manufacturer (historically operated within the Synthesia chemical complex), state-owned Explosia a.s. is one of the few European plants capable of producing both single-base extruded (S-series) and double-base extruded and spherical (D-series) small-arms propellants on one site.22

Explosia markets its commercial catalog in Europe under the Lovex brand and serves as the primary OEM supplier for Shooters World in North America.23 Historically, Explosia also supplied Accurate Arms’ early Czech extruded rifle propellants (including early versions of Accurate 2015, 2495, 4064, 4350, and double-base extruded Accurate 5744) before Western Powders relocated several of those extruded contracts to North America.24

Historical Legacy Makers

Older manuals, vintage canisters, and archival ballistic literature frequently cite five historic manufacturers whose names no longer appear on new commercial rifle production or have been absorbed into modern groups:

  • DuPont (Carneys Point, New Jersey). Founded in 1802 as a black powder mill on Brandywine Creek, DuPont built the Carneys Point smokeless works in the 1890s and engineered the entire classic IMR rifle lineage.25 DuPont spun off its commercial IMR sporting powder business into the independent IMR Powder Company in 1986, with extrusion shifting permanently to Valleyfield, Quebec.2
  • Hercules Powder Company (Kenvil, New Jersey & Radford, Virginia). Created in 1912 by the U.S. federal antitrust breakup of DuPont, Hercules inherited Laflin & Rand’s double-base smokeless expertise (Bullseye, Unique, HiVel, 2400) and operated the government-owned Radford Army Ammunition Plant from World War II onward.26 In 1995, Alliant Techsystems (ATK) acquired Hercules Aerospace—including its sporting propellant division—rebranding the canister line as Alliant Powder.27
  • Olin Corporation / Western Cartridge Company (East Alton, Illinois). Developed Fred Olsen’s spherical Ball Powder process in 1933 and acquired Winchester Repeating Arms in 1931; Olin relocated commercial and military ball-powder production from East Alton to its new St. Marks, Florida facility in 1970 before divesting St. Marks to General Dynamics in 1999.5
  • Nobel / ICI (Ardeer, Scotland). Founded by Alfred Nobel in 1871 as the British Dynamite Company (later Nobel Industries and Imperial Chemical Industries), the vast Ardeer complex manufactured commercial extruded propellants for Hodgdon—including post-surplus H4831 and H4895—during the late 20th century before Hodgdon consolidated extruded sourcing at ADI Mulwala.28
  • SNPE / Nobel Sport (France — Vectan). Descended from France’s historic Service des Poudres (where Paul Vieille invented Poudre B in 1884), SNPE commercialized the Vectan Tubal single-base extruded rifle powders and Ba/Sp lines.29 Today, Eurenco manages France’s industrial energetics heritage while Nobel Sport distributes Vectan retail canisters, sourcing its spherical Sp powders from PB Clermont.21

Major Commercial Canister Brands and Packagers

Six commercial organizations account for almost the entire retail bottleneck rifle powder market across North America and Europe:

  • Hodgdon Powder Company (Shawnee, Kansas). Founded in 1947 by Bruce Hodgdon on WWII surplus 4895, Hodgdon transformed from a regional salvager into North America’s dominant propellant packager through three strategic milestones: acquiring IMR Powder Company in 2003, securing the license to market and distribute Winchester smokeless powders in 2005, and acquiring Western Powders (bringing Accurate and Ramshot under its corporate roof) in 2020.30 Hodgdon operates no cellulose nitration plants of its own; instead, it sources across four primary OEMs—Thales/ADI Mulwala (Hodgdon Extreme, IMR 8208 XBR), GD-OTS Valleyfield (traditional IMR, contracted Accurate extruded), GD St. Marks Powder (Winchester, Hodgdon spherical), and PB Clermont (Ramshot, Accurate spherical).31
  • Alliant Powder (Anoka, Minnesota). Descended from Hercules Powder Company via ATK (1995), Vista Outdoor (2015), and The Kinetic Group (2024), Alliant packages modern bottleneck rifle propellants sourced from three primary OEMs: Eurenco Bofors (Sweden), Nitrochemie (Switzerland/Germany), and GD St. Marks Powder (USA, Power Pro series).32
  • Norma Precision AB (Åmotfors, Sweden). Founded in Norway in 1902 and established in Åmotfors in 1911 (later owned by RUAG Ammotec and acquired by Beretta Holding in 2022), Norma packages Bofors-manufactured extruded rifle propellants tailored to European and international precision cartridges.33
  • Shooters World (Charlotte, North Carolina). Founded in 2013 as a North American propellant importer and packager, Shooters World partners directly with Czech OEM Explosia a.s. to distribute Lovex single-base and double-base rifle propellants under descriptive English retail names.34
  • Reload Swiss (Wimmis, Switzerland). Launched in 2013 as Nitrochemie Wimmis AG’s factory-direct commercial brand, Reload Swiss markets Swiss-made single-base and EI® impregnated propellants across Europe and export markets without an intermediary packager.18
  • Vihtavuori (Laukaa, Finland). Operated directly by OEM Nammo Vihtavuori Oy, selling Finnish-made N100, N500, and heavy-caliber propellants globally (distributed in the United States through Capstone Precision Group).12
