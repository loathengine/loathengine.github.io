Chapter 7. High-Efficiency, REACH, and Extreme Long Range (2006–Present)

Key points

  • Two distinct European technologies redefined modern extruded rifle powders: Nitrochemie EI® (Switzerland/Germany) in Reloder 17 (2008), Reloder 26/33/50, and Reload Swiss (2013), and Eurenco Bofors TZ® (Sweden) in AR-Comp (2011), Reloder 23 (2014), Reloder 16 (2016), and Reloder TS 15.5 (2021).
  • Decoppering chemistry and thermally stabilized spherical propellants transformed U.S. ball powders, from Hodgdon CFE 223 (2012) to Winchester StaBALL 6.5 (announced late 2019, retail 2020) and StaBALL Match / HD (January 2023).
  • Extreme Long Range (ELR) and modern match cartridges drove specialized Vihtavuori releases—N570 (2010), N565 (2018), N555 (2020), and N568 (2021)—alongside ADI’s IMR 8208 XBR (2010).
  • EU REACH enforcement against 2,4-DNT and DBP (~2018) halted European Union imports of ADI/Hodgdon Extreme and legacy IMR powders, while leaving U.S. and Australian markets unaffected.
  • Supply-chain shocks—including the 2015 IMR 4007 SSC and 2019 Valleyfield Accurate 2495/4064/4350 stability recalls, the August 2022 discontinuation of IMR Enduron, Hodgdon’s 2020 acquisition of Western Powders, and post-2020 defense procurement surges—remade global canister availability.

High-energy impregnated and temperature-stabilized extrusions

Entering the late 2000s, precision riflemen wanted a seemingly contradictory combination of traits: the high muzzle velocity of nitroglycerin-boosted propellants, the flat thermal curve of Australian single-base extrusions, clean copper-free barrels, and compliance with tightening European chemical regulations. Two European defense manufacturers answered with radically different—and frequently confused—proprietary processes.

Nitrochemie EI®: Reloder 17, Reloder 26/33/50, and Reload Swiss

In Switzerland and Germany, Nitrochemie (Nitrochemie Wimmis AG and Nitrochemie Aschau GmbH, a joint venture of Rheinmetall and RUAG) developed EI® (Extrusion Impregnation) technology.1 Rather than applying a thin surface retardant only to the outside skin of a finished grain, Nitrochemie’s EI® process impregnates nitroglycerin and burn-rate-moderating deterrents deep into the extruded nitrocellulose matrix along a controlled radial gradient (see Chapter 2).1 That radial gradient broadens the pressure plateau, allowing a rifle cartridge to sustain higher average pressure during bullet travel without exceeding peak chamber limits.

The technology debuted in North American canisters in 2008 as Alliant Reloder 17, immediately demonstrating notable velocity gains over traditional single-base propellants in short magnums and mid-bore match cartridges.2 Alliant soon partnered with Nitrochemie to extend the EI® family into high-capacity magnums and extreme long-range cartridges with Reloder 26, Reloder 33, and Reloder 50 (for .50 BMG and heavy ELR wildcats).3

Seeing strong civilian demand for its chemistry, Nitrochemie launched its own direct retail brand—Reload Swiss—in 2013.1 Organized on a clean numeric burn-rate ladder (RS36 through RS80 for bottleneck rifles), Reload Swiss gave European and international handloaders direct access to both EI®-impregnated formulations (such as RS60, the direct Swiss counterpart to Reloder 17, alongside RS52 and RS70) and cooler-burning single-base companions (RS50, RS62).1

Eurenco Bofors TZ®: AR-Comp, Reloder 23, Reloder 16, and TS 15.5

Independently in Karlskoga, Sweden, Eurenco Bofors AB developed TZ® (Temperature Zone) technology—a separate Swedish chemical-stabilization architecture engineered to eliminate thermal velocity drift across extreme temperatures while removing restricted DNT and DBP additives and incorporating decoppering compounds.4

(Industry discussions frequently misattribute TZ® powders to Switzerland’s Nitrochemie. In fact, TZ® is a registered trademark of Sweden’s Eurenco Bofors AB, whereas EI® belongs to Nitrochemie.)

Alliant deployed Bofors TZ® chemistry across four major rifle introductions over a single decade:

  • AR-Comp (2011): A clean-burning, thermally stabilized medium-speed powder tailored for gas-operated .223/5.56mm and .308/7.62mm rifles.3
  • Reloder 23 (announced 2014; retail 2014–2015): A magnum-class successor in the Reloder 22 burn-rate zone engineered to cure the thermal sensitivity of older double-base magnum powders while reducing copper fouling.3
  • Reloder 16 (2016): Launched at the 2016 SHOT Show directly into the fiercely contested 4350 burn-rate bracket, quickly becoming a favorite for 6.5 Creedmoor, 6mm Creedmoor, and .270 Winchester competitors.3
  • Reloder TS 15.5 (2021): Positioned specifically between Reloder 15 and Reloder 16 for heavy-bullet .308 Winchester, .223 Remington, and mid-bore match rifles.3

Decoppering additives and temperature-stable ball powders

While European factories refined high-efficiency extruded grains, American and Australian plants tackled two other persistent bottlenecks: copper jacket fouling and the historic temperature sensitivity of spherical ball propellants.

IMR 8208 XBR (2010) and Hodgdon CFE 223 (2012)

In 2010, Hodgdon partnered again with ADI Mulwala (BM8208) to launch IMR 8208 XBR—a finely cut, temperature-insensitive single-base extruded powder engineered specifically for benchrest precision, .223 Remington heavy-bullet service rifles, and .308 Winchester Palma loads.5 Despite wearing the IMR label, 8208 XBR came from Australia rather than Valleyfield, demonstrating how fully modern brand labels had decoupled from OEM geography.

Two years later, in 2012, Hodgdon and St. Marks Powder brought military-derived decoppering chemistry into mainstream commercial canisters with Hodgdon CFE 223.6 Incorporating metallic copper-fouling-eraser (CFE) additives (tin/bismuth-class compounds originally developed to curb coppering in rapid-fire military cannons), CFE 223 minimized jacket build-up inside rifled bores while retaining the high bulk density and smooth metering of a spherical powder (see Chapter 2).6

The Winchester StaBALL breakthrough (2019–2023)

For more than sixty years, riflemen had treated a basic engineering tradeoff as permanent: extruded powders offered superior thermal stability, while spherical ball powders offered superior metering and higher charge density at the cost of greater temperature drift.

In October 2019 (reaching retail shelves in 2020), Hodgdon and St. Marks Powder broke that dichotomy with Winchester StaBALL 6.5—the first temperature-insensitive spherical rifle propellant formulated specifically for bottleneck cartridges in the 4350 burn-rate class (such as 6.5 Creedmoor, .243 Winchester, and .270 Winchester).7 StaBALL 6.5 combined ball-powder metering, copper-fouling reduction, and REACH-compliant chemistry without DNT or DBP.7

At the January 2023 SHOT Show, Winchester expanded the stabilized spherical platform across both ends of the bottleneck spectrum, unveiling StaBALL Match (faster, targeting .223 Remington, .308 Winchester, and 6mm match cases) and StaBALL HD (slower, targeting 6.5 PRC, 7mm PRC, .300 PRC, and overbore magnums).7

Extreme Long Range and modern competition releases

During the 2010s and early 2020s, cartridge design shifted decisively toward high-ballistic-coefficient bullets fired from efficient, steeply shouldered cases: the 6.5 Creedmoor, 6mm Dasher, 6.5 PRC, 7mm PRC, .300 PRC, .300 Norma Magnum, .338 Lapua Magnum, and .375/.416 CheyTac. These cartridges pushed demand for slow-burning, high-density, thermally stable propellants with anti-copper additives.

Finland’s Vihtavuori answered with a targeted modernization of its N500 high-energy family:

  • N570 (2010): A slow, high-energy coarse extruded powder that became a defining Extreme Long Range benchmark in .300 Norma Magnum, .338 Lapua Magnum, and heavy-bullet magnums.8
  • N565 (2018): Engineered specifically for military and competitive .338 Lapua Magnum and 7mm/.300 magnum loads, pairing high density with decoppering chemistry and improved thermal stability.8
  • N555 (2020): Purpose-built for the 6.5 Creedmoor and mid-capacity precision cartridges, featuring low temperature sensitivity and an anti-copper agent in the 4350 burn-rate window.8
  • N568 (2021): A slower-burning companion between N565 and N570 tailored for high-capacity PRC and magnum cases running heavy modern projectiles.8

During the same decade, Shooters World entered the U.S. retail canister market around 2015 as the dedicated American packager for Czech Explosia / Lovex propellants (Precision Rifle, Match Rifle, SW4350, Long Rifle), expanding commercial access to Central European single-base and double-base lines.9

Regulatory, chemical-stability, and supply-chain shocks

The modern era has demonstrated that propellant history is no longer driven solely by ballistic innovation. Environmental regulation, chemical aging failures, corporate consolidation, and geopolitical defense procurement now reshape commercial powder shelves just as powerfully as chemistry.

EU REACH enforcement (2018)

Under the European Union’s REACH (Registration, Evaluation, Authorisation and Restriction of Chemicals) framework, the European Chemicals Agency (ECHA) placed 2,4-dinitrotoluene (2,4-DNT) and dibutyl phthalate (DBP)—two classic twentieth-century propellant deterrents and plasticizers—onto its Annex XIV Authorisation List as substances of very high concern.10

Once sunset dates and import enforcement took full effect across Europe around 2018, commercial imports of traditional Thales/ADI (Hodgdon Extreme) propellants (including Varget, H4350, H4895, H4831SC, and IMR 8208 XBR) and legacy IMR powders were blocked from the European Union market.10 Importantly, North American and Australian markets remained unaffected by EU REACH, where those propellants continued to be sold legally. Inside Europe, however, the regulatory wall accelerated a permanent migration toward REACH-compliant European propellants from Vihtavuori, Reload Swiss, Norma, Eurenco Bofors TZ®, and Explosia/Lovex.

The rise and 2022 discontinuation of IMR Enduron

Anticipating REACH rules and growing demand for thermally stable, decoppering extruded propellants, Hodgdon and GD-OTS Canada (Valleyfield) introduced the IMR Enduron series starting in 2015—spanning IMR 4166, IMR 4451, IMR 4955, IMR 7977, and IMR 8133.11 Formulated without DNT or DBP, Enduron combined temperature insensitivity with copper-fouling-eraser additives in a Canadian-made extruded line.

Despite technical merit, persistent supplier and manufacturing constraints—coupled with product-line streamlining as Winchester StaBALL expanded—led Hodgdon to discontinue the entire IMR Enduron series in August 2022, just two years after discontinuing legacy staples Hodgdon H414 and IMR 4320 in 2020.11

Modern chemical-stability recalls (2015 and 2019)

Two high-profile commercial recalls reminded the industry that nitric ester decomposition (see Chapter 12) is not merely a nineteenth-century naval hazard:

  • IMR 4007 SSC (September 2, 2015): Hodgdon issued a safety recall covering six lots of IMR 4007 SSC after identifying rapid chemical deterioration capable of causing self-heating and spontaneous combustion; the powder was subsequently discontinued.12
  • Accurate 2495, 4064, and 4350 (November 2019): Western Powders and GD-OTS Canada Valleyfield recalled specific 1-pound and 8-pound lots manufactured prior to October 1, 2016, after a stabilizer/chemical processing defect caused accelerated propellant degradation—evidenced by lid deformation, discoloration, pungent acidic fumes, red-brown nitrogen dioxide dust, and spontaneous ignition risk.13
Safety

Smokeless propellants are energetic nitric esters that depend on finite sacrificial stabilizers to prevent autocatalytic breakdown. Any canister showing acrid acidic odor, reddish-brown dust or fumes, clumping, or unexplained warmth—or matching an active manufacturer recall notice—must be removed from service immediately and rendered inert according to manufacturer safety instructions (see Chapter 12).14

Corporate consolidation and defense-driven shortages

The commercial distribution landscape consolidated sharply at the start of the 2020s. In October 2020, Hodgdon Powder Company acquired Western Powders, bringing the Accurate and Ramshot rifle brands under the same corporate roof as Hodgdon, IMR, and the Winchester powder license.15 Meanwhile, Alliant Powder moved through Vista Outdoor’s corporate restructuring into The Kinetic Group in 2023–2024.

At the same time, surging global defense demand for nitrocellulose, nitroglycerin, and finished artillery and small-arms propellants during the 2020–2025 period exposed the structural fragility of civilian supply. Because the same eight primary industrial plants supply both national militaries and civilian canisters (see Chapter 9), wartime artillery procurement rapidly diverted plant capacity away from commercial packaging—leading to multi-year retail shortages of Alliant Reloder lines and European extruded propellants across North America and Europe alike.
