What this treatise is—and is not

This volume is a standalone historical and technical reference on smokeless rifle propellants. It is written for readers who already grasp the basic purpose of smokeless powder and want an accurate desk reference on how propellants evolved, how industrial plants manufacture and coat them, who actually produces the powders sold under commercial canister labels, why legacy numerical designations repeat across brands, and how individual bottleneck rifle propellants compare in chemistry, lineage, and empirical burn rate.

It is not a reloading manual. It contains no charge-weight tables, starting or maximum loads, load-workup recipes, reloading-bench procedures, barrel-harmonic tuning claims, or ballistic-software calibration walkthroughs. Whenever two commercial powders share an OEM plant or a close burn-rate rank, this treatise documents their relationship while explaining why lot-to-lot variance and independent packager specifications prohibit blind grain-for-grain substitution.

The historical narrative in Part I and early Part II opens broadly—covering black powder and the early military, pistol, and shotgun milestones where smokeless chemistry and corporate lineages overlapped—before narrowing firmly to bottleneck rifle propellants. Bottleneck cartridges operate across wide case-to-bore ratios, high chamber pressures, long barrels, and severe thermal demands, making them the segment where propellant chemistry, grain geometry, and surface engineering reach their highest complexity.

Reading paths by question

You may read this treatise sequentially from Chapter 1 or jump straight to the section that answers your question:

  • Chemistry, manufacturing, and internal ballistics. Read Part I (Chapters 1–3) for nitration chemistry, stabilizers, extruded versus spherical production, surface deterrents, thermochemical parameters, and bottleneck combustion physics.
  • Propellant history and introduction timelines. Read Part II (Chapters 4–7) for the chronological evolution from Poudre B (1884) through WWII surplus, the Australian temperature-insensitive revolution, modern impregnated and stabilized propellants, and EU REACH disruptions.
  • Naming lineages, OEM plants, and commercial packagers. Read Part III (Chapters 8–10) to untangle the 4350, 4895, 4831, and 4064 families, profile the world’s major industrial plants, and consult the OEM twins, sister families, and recall tables.
  • Cartridge matching, lot variance, and chemical aging. Read Part IV (Chapters 11–12) for burn-rate windows across bottleneck cartridge classes, physical causes of lot drift, moisture equilibrium, nitric ester decomposition kinetics, and field inspection signs.
  • Empirical burn-rate lookup or individual powder research. Turn directly to Part V for the complete empirical burn-rate reference table and the alphabetical-by-brand Propellant Compendium.
  • Quick dates, unit conversions, and definitions. Consult the Back Matter for the Master Chronological Timeline (Appendix A), the 1-page Safety Card (Appendix B), Thermochemical Conversions (Reference), Notes, Sources, Glossary, and Index.

Using the Part V Compendium

Part V is divided into two linked sections. Section A presents an empirical relative burn-rate reference compiled from multi-cartridge published test data, ordered from fastest to slowest, alongside OEM plant attribution, grain class, bulk density, heat of explosion, and page pointers into the Compendium.

Section B profiles four foundational historical propellants followed by individual bottleneck rifle propellants grouped by brand with right-edge thumb tabs. Each Compendium entry pairs a concise narrative history (introduction timeline, naming lineage, chemistry, OEM twins or sister families, and bottleneck role) on the left with a standardized Specification Card on the right. Each active database powder card carries a compact QR code and direct URL to its live technical record at empiricalprecision.com.

Safety and evidence callouts

Two structured callouts appear sparingly through the narrative chapters:

  • Safety callouts highlight genuine energetic hazards such as autocatalytic decomposition, thermal storage limits, warning signs of degraded powder, and the risk of substituting powders by burn-rate chart or OEM cross-reference.
  • Evidence and Contested callouts examine recurring technical claims where physics and controlled test data separate fact from oversimplification—for example, whether base chemistry alone predicts temperature sensitivity, or whether confirmed OEM twins behave identically across separate production lots.
