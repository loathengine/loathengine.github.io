What you will learn

You will learn how to set up a precision rifle and optic, build repeatable marksmanship positions, read wind and distance, evaluate cartridges and ammunition with measured statistics, model external ballistics and hit probability, and set honest terminal-performance limits in the field.

This book starts with known-good (safe, functional) ammunition—whether commercial match ammunition or handloads. You do not need to handload to use this book. If measured analysis shows that a handload falls short of your goal, take that data back to the workshop (see The Handloading Handbook). If factory ammunition falls short, test another lot or cartridge using the same protocol.

What is covered and what is not

Centerfire and rimfire (.22 LR and other rimfire) rifles in bolt-action and semi-automatic platforms are covered. External ballistics and shooting workflows focus on Benchrest, PRS and practical precision, Service Rifle, and ethical long-range hunting. Measurement science applies equally to other accuracy disciplines such as bullseye pistol. Action-shooting formats that do not rely on external ballistics, such as 3-Gun, are out of scope. Ammunition manufacturing and reloading bench steps are covered in the standalone companion volume, Empirical Precision: The Handloading Handbook — Ammunition Manufacturing and Function Testing.

Where to start

You can read this handbook straight through, or follow the path that matches your goal:

  • New precision shooter: Start at Chapter 1 and read straight through.
  • 100-meter Benchrest shooter or factory-ammunition evaluator: Focus on Chapters 1–8, 10–16, and 23–24.
  • Hunter with a verified load needing a DOPE card and ethical hunting range: Focus on Chapters 2, 10–11, 18, 20, and 22 (plus Chapter 21 for hit-probability / WEZ math).
  • 1,000-yard F-Class, PRS, or Service Rifle competitor: Focus on Chapters 3–11, 14–21, and 23–24.
  • Rimfire (.22 LR) precision and trainers: Focus on Chapters 1 to 16, 17 (barrel tuners), 19 to 21, and 23 to 24.

This is how it is done. This is how it is easier on EP.

Empirical Precision (EP) is a free and open-source reloading and ballistics app at empiricalprecision.com. It works offline and needs no account. Each method is taught in full first, with formulas and worked examples. Where EP does the same job, an Easier on EP box names the tool and gives a direct link.

Safety and evidence tags

A Safety · See Chapter 2 box marks a step where a mistake can damage the rifle or hurt you. Every empirical claim with an open evidence question carries one of three tags: Evidence · Supported by data, Evidence · Plausible but unproven, or Evidence · Folklore. Contested topics follow one pattern: the claim, the physics, the test, the data, and the verdict.
