Chapter 22. Terminal Ballistics and Ethical Hunting Range

On this page (6 sections)
  • Why it matters
  • How it is done
  • Impact velocity and bullet construction
  • Impact energy, sectional density, and penetration
  • Setting an ethical maximum hunting range
  • Notes

Key points

  • Reliable bullet expansion depends on impact velocity, not muzzle velocity.
  • Cup-and-core, bonded, and monolithic copper bullets have different minimum expansion velocity thresholds.
  • Ethical maximum hunting range is the shorter of two limits: your field hit-probability limit (WEZ) and your bullet’s minimum expansion velocity distance.
  • Measure real field positional dispersion before committing to long-range shots on game.

Why it matters

External ballistics delivers the bullet to the target; terminal ballistics determines what happens when it arrives. Knowing both sets an honest, ethical distance limit in the field.

How it is done

Impact velocity and bullet construction

A hunting bullet does not know how fast it left the muzzle. It only knows how fast it is traveling when it strikes tissue. Aerodynamic drag slows every bullet continuously from the moment it leaves the bore. A typical 140 gr 6.5mm hunting bullet (G7 BC .315) launched at 2,700 fps drops to roughly 2,220 fps at 300 yards, 1,925 fps at 500 yards, and 1,785 fps at 600 yards on a standard day. Example numbers. Not load data.

Every expanding hunting bullet has a minimum reliable expansion velocity. Above that speed, hydraulic pressure inside the nose cavity peels the jacket or petals open, creating a wide wound channel. Below that speed, the bullet fails to open and passes through like a full-metal-jacket projectile, leaving a narrow wound track that risks a slow, unethical loss of game.

Construction sets the velocity window. Always check the bullet maker’s published minimum expansion velocity for your exact bullet, because jacket thickness and tip geometry vary even within the same class.

Bullet constructionTypical minimum expansion velocityStrengthsTrade-offs
Cup-and-core (lead core, copper jacket)Roughly 1,600 to 1,800 fpsOpens reliably at lower impact speeds and longer distancesCan lose weight or break up on close, high-velocity bone hits
Bonded (lead core bonded to jacket)Roughly 1,800 to 2,000 fpsHigh weight retention and deep penetration on heavy boneSlightly higher expansion floor than soft cup-and-core designs
Monolithic copper (solid copper or alloy)Roughly 1,800 to 2,200 fps (commonly near 2,000 fps)Retains nearly 100 percent of its weight; deep, straight penetration; lead-freeHarder metal needs higher impact speed to open reliably; longer for a given weight, which can require a faster twist (see Chapter 18)

A bullet that stays supersonic to 1,200 yards is not a 1,200-yard hunting bullet. Its impact velocity drops below the expansion floor hundreds of yards before it reaches the transonic zone.

Safety · See Chapter 2

Positively identify your target and what lies beyond it before every shot. Follow all local hunting regulations, including minimum caliber, energy, or non-lead projectile rules. Never take a shot on game at a distance you have not verified from realistic field positions.

Impact energy, sectional density, and penetration

Shooters often check kinetic energy (KE) as a rough guide to downrange power.

KE = w × v² ÷ 450,437

KE   kinetic energy in foot-pounds (ft-lbs)
w    bullet weight in grains
v    impact velocity in fps

Because velocity is squared, losing speed downrange cuts energy sharply. Using the 140 gr example bullet above, muzzle energy at 2,700 fps is 2,266 ft-lbs. At 300 yards (2,220 fps), energy has fallen to 1,532 ft-lbs. At 500 yards (1,925 fps), it is 1,152 ft-lbs. Example numbers. Not load data.

Kinetic energy alone does not cleanly harvest game. A bullet that hits the heart and lungs and expands properly at 1,000 ft-lbs is far more effective than a 2,500 ft-lbs bullet that clips the edge of the vitals or fails to open. Treat traditional energy rules of thumb (such as 1,000 ft-lbs for deer or 1,500 ft-lbs for elk) as rough sanity checks, not guarantees.

Penetration depends heavily on bullet construction and sectional density (see Chapter 19).

Sectional density = w ÷ (7,000 × d²)

w   bullet weight in grains
d   bullet diameter in inches

Between two bullets of the same construction, the one with higher sectional density packs more mass behind its frontal area. That extra mass helps it drive straight through hide, muscle, and heavy shoulder bone to reach the vitals. A 140 gr 6.5mm bullet (.264 inch) has a sectional density of 0.287, while a 120 gr bullet of the same diameter drops to 0.246.

Expansion and penetration pull in opposite directions. A bullet that opens very wide creates a larger initial wound channel, which increases drag in tissue and reduces penetration depth. A tough bonded or monolithic bullet opens more conservatively, holds its weight, and penetrates deeper through heavy game, but it requires higher impact velocity to start expanding. Match the bullet to the game’s body mass and your expected shot distances.

Setting an ethical maximum hunting range

Your ethical maximum hunting range is the shorter of two objective limits:

  1. The terminal limit. The distance where your bullet’s impact velocity falls to its minimum reliable expansion velocity (checked for the temperature and elevation of your hunt).
  2. The hit-probability limit. The distance where your first-round hit probability on the animal’s vital zone drops below your ethical threshold (commonly 90 percent or higher), measured from real field positions and honest wind-calling skill (see Chapter 21).

Do not measure your field dispersion from a concrete shooting bench with a front rest and rear bag. Measure it from the positions you actually use afield: prone over a pack or bipod, sitting off a tripod, or kneeling. Most shooters group roughly two to three times wider from field positions than they do from a shooting bench.

  1. Choose the vital-zone size for your game (for example, an 8-inch vital zone for deer-sized game).
  2. Measure your field positional dispersion with at least 10 to 20 shots from your actual hunting support. Convert that composite mean radius to a one-sigma angular spread (divide mean radius by 1.2533, see Chapter 21).
  3. Enter your trued trajectory, velocity SD, field dispersion, and honest field wind-call error into a Weapon Employment Zone model (see Chapter 21).
  4. Find the distance where vital-zone hit probability drops below 90 percent.
  5. Find the distance on your drop chart where impact velocity reaches the bullet maker’s minimum expansion velocity.
  6. Set your ethical maximum range at the shorter of those two distances.

Worked example. Example numbers. Not load data.

Take the 6.5 Creedmoor 140 gr load (G7 BC .315, 2,700 fps muzzle velocity, 12 fps velocity SD) on an 8-inch square vital zone (4 inches from center to each edge). From a pack and bipod in the field, the hunter shoots a one-sigma positional dispersion of 0.50 MOA (roughly 1.5 MOA 5-shot groups). Without wind flags, the hunter’s honest wind-call error is 2.0 mph one-sigma. Using the Chapter 19 wind-drift figures and Chapter 21’s root-sum-square error budget:

Two lessons stand out immediately:

  • With a cup-and-core bullet needing 1,800 fps, impact velocity holds nearly to 600 yards. Hit probability, however, drops below 90 percent past roughly 340 yards. Field dispersion and wind-call error set the ethical maximum range at about 340 yards, well inside the bullet’s terminal limit.
  • With a monolithic copper bullet needing 2,000 fps, impact velocity falls below the expansion floor near 450 yards. Even if a steadier position and calmer wind kept hit probability above 90 percent to 500 yards, the bullet’s expansion velocity would cap the ethical shot at 450 yards.

In both cases, the shorter limit governs. When conditions worsen, such as gusting wind, an awkward rest, or heavy breathing after a climb, shorten your maximum range further.

Evidence · Supported by data

Weapon Employment Zone modeling shows that realistic field dispersion and wind-call uncertainty typically cap ethical vital-zone hit probability well short of a modern hunting cartridge’s supersonic or energy limits.1
