What you will learn

You will learn to handload bottleneck rifle cartridges for bolt-action and semi-automatic rifles. You will learn every step for the workshop and every step for the range, and how to make each decision with measured data. The work ends with safe, functional ammunition that meets your velocity and consistency expectations, handing off to The Precision Rifle Handbook for extended statistical evaluation, external ballistics, DOPE cards, and hit probability.

What is not covered

Handgun cartridges, straight-wall rifle cartridges, shotshells and cast bullets are not covered. The methods for measuring and comparing loads still apply to them. The workshop steps and the pressure rules differ. Use a reloading manual with specific steps for those cartridges.1

Where to start

Start at Chapter 1. Each chapter builds on the ones before it. Where a step uses a later chapter, a cross-reference names it. Later, turn to whichever chapter fits the job at hand. Chapter 2 holds the safety rules behind every step. Do not skip it.

Some jobs need only part of the book first:

  • Before you load your first round, read Chapters 2 to 25, 28, and 30. For a semi-automatic, add Chapter 26.
  • Before you work up a load, read Chapters 2, 15, 16, 27, 28, and 30. For a semi-automatic, add Chapter 26.
  • For a hunter needing a safe load and a DOPE card, read Chapters 2, 15 to 25, 27, 28, and 30, then turn to The Precision Rifle Handbook Chapters 10, 11, 18, 20, and 22.
  • Before you choose between loads, read Chapters 27 to 29, then The Precision Rifle Handbook Chapters 14 to 16.
  • Before you shoot at distance, read The Precision Rifle Handbook Chapters 18 to 21.
  • When a lot, the season or the barrel changes, read Chapter 30.
  • When something goes wrong, start at Chapter 31.

This is how it is done. This is how it is easier on EP.

Empirical Precision (EP) is a reloading and ballistics app at empiricalprecision.com. It works offline and needs no account. Your data stays on your device unless you choose to send it. EP is free and open source, so there will never be a charge to use it.

Each method is taught in full with step-by-step instructions. Where there is math, you also get the formulas and a worked example. You can do every job with a notebook, a calculator or a spreadsheet (except the drop chart, which needs a ballistic solver). Where EP can do the same job, an Easier on EP box says which of its tools does it and what you get. Each box includes a QR code and a direct link to that page. EP is a tool like a tumbler or a chronograph but never a requirement.

How each chapter is built

Chapters 1 to 31 use the same parts in the same order, so you always know where to look. The boxes appear only where they apply.

  • Key points. The main ideas kept brief.
  • Why it matters. The physics or the risk in plain words.
  • How it is done. The full method. Workshop and range tasks come as numbered steps. Math comes with formulas and a worked example.
  • Safety boxes. Warnings that prevent injury or rifle damage, cross-referenced to Chapter 2.
  • Evidence boxes. What the data says about contested or unproven claims.
  • Easier on EP boxes. The EP tool for the same job with a QR code and direct link.

Chapter 31 ends with short answers to common questions. Each answer points to the chapter that teaches the method in full.

Unit conversions, Appendices A and B, the manufacturer directory, the notes for each chapter, the glossary, the sources and the master index are at the back of the book. A small raised number in the text points to its note.

Safety tags

Never ignore a safety rule. A Safety · See Chapter 2 box marks a step where a mistake can damage the rifle or hurt you.

Evidence tags

Handloading carries a lot of habit and dogma. Some of it is proven. Some of it is plausible. Some of it is wrong. This manual gives each technique with an open evidence question one of three evidence tags.

TagWhat it means
Evidence · Supported by dataControlled tests or large samples support it. A note gives the source.
Evidence · Plausible but unprovenThe physics suggests it may help. No public test with enough shots has shown it.
Evidence · FolkloreTested and not supported, or at odds with the physics.

Contested topics follow one pattern: the claim, the physics, the test, the data and the verdict. A tag can change when new data arrives. Your own data counts too. You can test a claim yourself with enough shots to settle it (see The Precision Rifle Handbook).

Example numbers and units

Worked examples use velocities, group sizes and measured lengths. They never use a charge weight for a real cartridge, bullet and powder. Every example is labeled “Example numbers. Not load data.”

Charges are in grains (gr) and velocities in feet per second (fps). Lengths in the text read like “.002 inch”. Pressures are in psi. SAAMI and C.I.P. are the two bodies that set cartridge standards, and C.I.P. states its limits in bar (see Chapter 4). Part IX gives a C.I.P. limit in bar. The Part X tables give every pressure in psi, C.I.P. cartridges included. Cross-references read like “(see Chapter 28)”.

In formulas, × means times, ÷ means divided by, x² means x squared, √ means the square root and Σ means the sum of. ≈ means about equal to and ± means plus or minus. π, read pi, is about 3.1416, and sin(x) is the sine of the angle x. A power that is not a whole number uses ^, so x^1.83 is x to the power 1.83, and exp(x) is e to the power x. σ, read sigma, is a standard deviation. Spreadsheet formulas keep the spreadsheet’s own symbols, such as * and SQRT().

Parts VII to X · Component reference and simulated ladders

Parts VII, VIII and IX contain the projectile directory, the combined propellant specifications, the relative burn rate order table, and the SAAMI and C.I.P. cartridge dimensions and pressure limits.

Part X holds the simulated load ladders from the Ignition internal ballistics engine. Each cartridge opens with a short history and a specification card from the SAAMI or C.I.P. standard. Next are tables ordered by bullet weight that include the cartridge overall length (COAL), representative bullet length, seating depth and usable and total case water capacity.

Each row in the table shows simulated charge and velocity at five steps, from step 1 (90 percent of step 5) to step 5 (the limit). Step 5 also includes simulated peak pressure in psi and case fill. Step 5 stops where predicted pressure reaches the SAAMI limit or the powder fills 105 percent of usable case capacity, whichever comes first. Where SAAMI sets no limit, step 5 uses the C.I.P. limit converted to psi. A fill over 100 percent is a compressed charge. The rows are predictions, not load data. Part X opens with how these simulations are computed and how to compare them against published manuals. Always develop your load from the component maker’s current published data, starting at the published start load and never exceeding the published maximum (see Chapters 15 and 28). Check the errata of the manual you load from and the component makers’ online data for anything newer than your copy.
