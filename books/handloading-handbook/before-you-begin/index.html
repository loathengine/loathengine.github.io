Front Matter· Essential Safety Rules

Before You Begin

These rules apply to every load you make. Read them before your first session. They are repeated as a card in Appendix B.

In the workshop

  1. Load only when you can give the job your full attention. Never load when tired, ill, hurried or after drinking.
  2. Wear safety glasses whenever you load.
  3. Keep one powder and one primer type on the workbench. Put everything else away.
  4. Use published load data for your exact components. Never substitute a powder because its name or its place on a burn chart looks similar. Never load a powder you cannot identify.
  5. Start every new load at the published start load, never below it, and work up in small steps. If a source lists only a maximum, start at least 10 percent below it. Never exceed a published maximum.
  6. Work up again from the start load if you change the primer, case maker, bullet, powder, barrel or barrel length. For a new lot of the same component, record the lot and load 10 check rounds. If your charge is at or near the published maximum, load those check rounds one step lower. Load in bulk only after the check at the range.
  7. Discard cases with an incipient head separation ring, a split or an enlarged, loose primer pocket. Never load a case longer than its maximum case length.
  8. Verify your scale with a check weight at the start of every session.
  9. Look into every charged case under good light before you seat a bullet. A double charge or an empty case must never leave the workshop.
  10. If you seat a bullet deeper than the published cartridge overall length (COAL), deeper than your recipe, or into the lands (the raised ribs of the rifling), treat it as a new load and work up again from the start load. Measure the cartridge base to ogive (CBTO) and the COAL. Do not guess.
  11. At the end of each session, return unused powder to its original container and empty the powder measure completely.

At the range

  1. Wear eye and ear protection.
  2. Fire the steps of a work-up in order, lowest first, and chronograph every shot. Stop at the published maximum charge or at your velocity ceiling, whichever comes first. Your velocity ceiling is the published velocity at the maximum charge, lowered for a shorter barrel but never raised for a longer one. Stop at a step that gains far more velocity than the steps before it. After any stop, take the unfired rounds home and pull their bullets. Chronograph a new lot’s check rounds and compare their velocity with your records. If they run faster by more than twice the noise, the amount two averages can differ by chance (see Chapter 30), lower the charge and work up again from below.
  3. Stop at once at any pressure sign. Signs include a stiff bolt lift, an ejector mark and a flattened, cratered or pierced primer. In a semi-automatic, an ejector mark is a sign when it is new or heavier than at the start load (see Chapter 26). The absence of signs does not prove a load is safe.
  4. Keep loaded rounds out of the sun and out of a hot chamber. A load near the maximum on a mild day has no margin left for a hot day.
  5. If a shot sounds or feels wrong, stop. Unload the rifle and check the bore for a stuck bullet before you fire again. If a round fails to fire, keep the muzzle downrange for at least 30 seconds before you open the action.

In storage and afterward

  1. Store powder and primers in their original containers, cool and dry, away from heat, flame and children. Never store powder in a sealed or strong container such as a gun safe. Wash your hands and face after loading, shooting or handling fired cases. Do not eat, drink or smoke until you have washed.

With any software prediction

  1. Every predicted pressure is for learning. It is never intended to replace published data. Never change a model’s powder settings to match your chronograph, since that moves its predicted pressure too.
